How “on 2.0” is decided. The timesheet tables record no UI attribution — an entry does not say whether it came from 1.0 or 2.0. So hours are split by each tenant's own TT 2.0 enable date: a week is counted as 2.0 once its Monday falls on or after that tenant's enable date, and pre-2.0 before it. A week containing the cutover is counted as pre-2.0, so the 2.0 series is slightly conservative.
— tenants have no recoverable enable date (enabled before LaunchDarkly's audit-log retention window, ~mid-Apr 2026) — their hours are shown as a separate “unattributed” band and are never counted as 2.0. Dual Fuel is the largest of these, so the unattributed band is big by volume, not by uncertainty about the tenant.
Corrupt rows are capped. Any single employee-day above 24h is clipped to 24 — this removes ~22,000 impossible hours per week from TJW Industrial's Feb–Jun history and similar spikes elsewhere. The most recent week is partial and drawn dashed.
Why this tab reads lower than the Tenants tab. The Tenants table shows — h on 2.0; this tab totals — h. Both are right for what they measure. The table counts from the exact enable date and uses raw durations. This tab assigns whole weeks (a cutover week counts as pre-2.0) and applies the 24h/employee-day cap. The gap is that conservatism plus the capped corrupt hours — treat the — h table figure as the headline and this tab as the shape of the trend.

Is 2.0 working?

The charts below measure the commitments TT 2.0 was signed up to, rather than how much time is flowing through it. The before/after scorecard is the summary — same tenants, either side of their own cutover. Review outcomes puts that on a weekly trend and tests the pain points in LEP-111 — that initiative exists because reviewers could not approve or edit across many employees at once. Payroll integrity is the closest computable stand-in for the Cohort 1 criterion that every tenant complete a full payroll run. Capability adoption separates using 2.0 from using 1.0 in a new skin. Report rate is the migration plan's own monthly confidence gate. Everything from Weekly labor hours onward is volume and data integrity — necessary, but it cannot tell you whether 2.0 is better.

Review outcomes — migrated tenants vs the ones still on 1.0

Share of time entries ending Approved, Dismissed, or still open, by week. Columns are tenants already on 2.0 that week; the dashed line is the same weeks for tenants that had not cut over yet — a live control group, same weeks, same product. A falling dismissal rate is the strongest evidence 2.0 improved review: time is being captured correctly the first time instead of being thrown out. Dismissal is read from the timesheet's dismissed flag, not its approval status: 1.0 records a dismissal by setting that flag and leaving the status as Pending, so reading the status instead understates 1.0 dismissals about sixteenfold and inverts this comparison. Approval speed is deliberately absent — see the note below the chart.

Which time-tracking flows is each tenant actually using?

Share of each tenant's post-cutover entries by flow. General Time (which replaced Daily Shift) and project time with no visit are genuinely new in 2.0. Time card sign-off and Daily Report project time both had 1.0 equivalents, and are here for contrast. 2.0's meal and rest break attestation settings are not shown: nothing records them per entry, only per tenant. A tenant using none of the new flows is on 2.0 but working exactly as it did on 1.0, which is a training conversation rather than a product failure.

Report rate against the 25% confidence gate

By the month a tenant migrated: the share that have since filed a High or Blocker Time Tracking & Labor support ticket. The migration plan's gate is that no more than 25% of a month's migrated tenants carry a High/Blocker report. Tickets attribute to tenants by id rather than by customer name, and count only from that tenant's own cutover onward. Small cohorts move this a long way — one tenant in a cohort of four is 25% on its own.

What counts as “migrated” here. The numerator is production Salesforce customer tenants carrying the time-tracking-redesign flag — the same set the Tenants tab lists — counted from the plan's own start date. — tenants were already on 2.0 before then (the original reference cohort, which sits outside the — population); counting them would show the pilot weeks running ahead of a schedule that never claimed them. Also excluded: the — internal training/demo tenants on the flag, which are sandboxes belonging to customers scheduled later, not migrations.

Two things cut the other way, and neither rescues the number. The plan's first 15 pilot entries include DNU/QA tenants with no Salesforce account, so they are invisible here — worth up to ~15. And a stricter reading, tenants actually logging hours on 2.0, gives — rather than — on the flag. The shortfall is an order of magnitude larger than either adjustment.
