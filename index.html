How “on 2.0” is decided. The timesheet tables record no UI attribution — an entry does not say whether it came from 1.0 or 2.0. So hours are split by each tenant's own TT 2.0 enable date: a week is counted as 2.0 once its Monday falls on or after that tenant's enable date, and pre-2.0 before it. A week containing the cutover is counted as pre-2.0, so the 2.0 series is slightly conservative.
Seven tenants have no recoverable enable date (enabled before LaunchDarkly's audit-log retention window, ~mid-Apr 2026) — their hours are shown as a separate “unattributed” band and are never counted as 2.0. Dual Fuel is the largest of these, so the unattributed band is big by volume, not by uncertainty about the tenant.
Corrupt rows are capped. Any single employee-day above 24h is clipped to 24 — this removes ~22,000 impossible hours per week from TJW Industrial's Feb–Jun history and similar spikes elsewhere. The most recent week is partial and drawn dashed.
Why this tab reads lower than the Tenants tab. The Tenants table shows 24,008 h on 2.0; this tab totals 16,502 h. Both are right for what they measure. The table counts from the exact enable date and uses raw durations. This tab assigns whole weeks (a cutover week counts as pre-2.0) and applies the 24h/employee-day cap. The gap is that conservatism plus the capped corrupt hours — treat 24,008 as the headline and this tab as the shape of the trend.
